# Phase 3 — Sensor Frame, Orientation & Alignment Validation
_Generated by `idr 0.1.0` at 2026-08-31T15:52:18.238845+00:00 from `data\processed\io_vnbd`. 154 canonical segment(s) analysed in 178.3s._

## 1. What this phase established, and what it did not
Phase 3 defines the coordinate frames, implements the rotation mathematics, estimates gravity and orientation, and recovers a phone→vehicle alignment with an explicit confidence.

**It does not validate that alignment against ground truth, because no ground truth exists.** The true phone→vehicle rotation for IO-VNBD is documented nowhere and was never independently measured, so nothing in the real-data sections below is an accuracy claim. Accuracy is established only against synthetic data, where the applied rotation is known by construction (section 6).

No position or velocity is propagated, no filter state or covariance exists, and no model is trained. Those begin in Phase 4.

## 2. Frame conventions
Full statement in `docs/architecture/sensor_frame_conventions.md`; the equations are in `docs/mathematics/phase3_orientation.md`. In brief:

| Frame | Axes | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Navigation | X east, Y north, Z up (ENU) | Local tangent plane |
| Vehicle | X forward, Y left, Z up | Right-handed; a left turn gives +Y lateral acceleration |
| Phone | X, Y, Z as delivered by the logger | Positions, **not** verified physical directions |

Rotations are named for the frames they connect, destination first: `R_vehicle_phone` maps phone coordinates to vehicle coordinates, and composition chains as `R_nav_phone = R_nav_vehicle @ R_vehicle_phone`. The type system enforces it — composing in the wrong order raises rather than returning a plausible wrong matrix.

Quaternions are Hamilton, stored `[x, y, z, w]`, and represent **active** rotations. Euler angles are Z-Y-X intrinsic and are used only for diagnostics.

## 3. Numerical tolerances
Each is chosen for the operation it guards, in float64.

| Tolerance | Value | Guards |
|---|---|---|
| `QUATERNION_NORM_TOLERANCE` | 1e-09 | ‖q‖ − 1; a norm costs ~6 rounding steps (~1e-15) |
| `ORTHONORMALITY_TOLERANCE` | 1e-09 | max\|RᵀR − I\|; nine dot products of three terms |
| `DETERMINANT_TOLERANCE` | 1e-09 | det(R) − 1; separates a rotation from a reflection |
| `ROUND_TRIP_TOLERANCE` | 1e-12 | quaternion→matrix→quaternion; measured worst case 8.7e-16 rad |
| `EULER_GIMBAL_LIMIT_RAD` | 1e-07 | how close \|pitch\| may come to 90° before roll/yaw stop separating |

One tolerance changed during development for a reason worth recording. `ROUND_TRIP_TOLERANCE` was 1e-8 while `angular_distance` used `2·acos(|a·b|)`. `acos` has an infinite derivative at 1, so two rotations differing by one ulp measured as ~1e-8 apart — a floor belonging to the *measurement*, not the round trip. Switching to `2·atan2(‖vec(a⁻¹b)‖, |w(a⁻¹b)|)` dropped the measured worst case to 8.7e-16, and the tolerance was tightened by four orders of magnitude to match.

## 4. Gravity estimation
An accelerometer measures **specific force**, not gravity. The two coincide only at rest, which is what makes the naive reading dangerous: under braking or on a rough road, treating the accelerometer as vertical tilts the whole downstream frame.

A sample counts as quasi-static when ‖a‖ is within 0.5 m/s² of 9.80665 m/s² **and** the angular rate is below 0.1 rad/s. Both conditions are needed: a steady turn holds ‖a‖ near 1 g while the direction sweeps round, and only the gyro test rejects it. At least 20 qualifying samples are required before any direction is claimed.

**Verified sign convention.** Both the accelerometer and the logger's gravity channel read specific force, so both point *away* from gravity at rest and both must be negated. This was measured, not assumed: across 66 real segments with a quasi-static interval, the median cosine between the two channels is **+0.999** and the gravity channel's magnitude is exactly 9.807 m/s². They agree, and the sign they agree on is Android's. An early implementation negated only one path; a synthetic round trip caught it as a **180° heading error**.

| Method | Segments | Median confidence |
|---|---|---|
| `gravity_channel` | 154 | 0.99 |

- Gravity was estimable in **154 of 154** segments.
- Median device tilt from the phone's −Z axis: **0.0°**. This describes how the devices were held; it is not a claim that the phone frame means what Android says.

**A documented blind spot.** The magnitude test cannot see acceleration perpendicular to gravity: adding *a* at right angles changes ‖a‖ only by `√(g² + a²) − g`, so a horizontal push up to about 3.1 m/s² passes the 0.5 m/s² gate. A window that accelerates consistently one way will therefore bias the vertical. The reported direction spread and confidence are what expose it, and a regression test pins the behaviour so it cannot change unnoticed.

## 5. Orientation and the magnetometer
A complementary filter, not a Kalman filter — a state vector with a covariance is Phase 4/5 work. The gyroscope is integrated with the exact axis-angle exponential over **actual** Δt, and pulled toward measured gravity at 0.5 Hz and toward magnetic north at 0.05 Hz. The magnetic gain is an order of magnitude weaker because a magnetometer's in-vehicle failure mode is a *steady* bias, which a strong gain would track faithfully into the estimate.

Which sensor supports which axis is reported per segment rather than implied:

- **roll, pitch** — `OBSERVED` whenever gravity is available;
- **yaw** — `OBSERVED` only with a trusted magnetometer, otherwise `DEAD_RECKONED` from the gyroscope (correct relatively, drifting absolutely), or `UNRESOLVED` with neither.

Confidence is capped at 0.5 whenever yaw has no absolute reference. An excellent roll and pitch with a free-running yaw is not a high-confidence 3-DOF orientation.

The magnetometer is checked for magnitude outside 25–65 µT, magnitude instability (a rigid rotation in a fixed field does not change ‖B‖), direction slewing faster than device motion explains, and inclination wander relative to gravity. Two independent findings drop it to zero weight; a field more than twice the plausible maximum is excluded on that ground alone, because it is not the Earth's field with noise on it.

- Magnetometer trusted at all in **86 of 154** segments (median weight 0.30).

## 6. Synthetic rotation recovery — the only accuracy claim here
A drive is generated in the vehicle frame, where forward is +X by construction, then rotated into a phone frame by a known rotation. The estimator must return that rotation. This is the only place accuracy can be measured, because it is the only place the answer is known.

| 30 | 0.087 |

| 60 | 0.087 |

Worst recovery error across the sweep: **0.087°**, with sensor noise applied. The sweep rotates about a general axis, so it exercises roll, pitch and yaw together rather than one at a time.

The test suite additionally recovers rotations about each individual axis at 0°, 15°, 30°, 45° and 90°, under sensor noise, under irregular sampling, with duplicate timestamps, and from the accelerometer alone with no gravity channel.

## 7. Phone→vehicle alignment on real data
Tilt and heading are not equally solvable, and the design keeps them apart:

- **Tilt (2 DOF)** comes from gravity and is available while stationary.
- **Heading (1 DOF)** requires motion. Rotating the phone about the vertical leaves every gravity reading identical, so a stationary calibration has no honest yaw. It is reported as unresolved, `rotation` is `None`, and applying such an estimate raises rather than silently rotating a caller's data by a placeholder.

Heading is recovered from longitudinal dynamics: the direction along which horizontal acceleration co-varies with dv/dt is the forward axis, and the sign of that covariance separates forward from reverse. A sample qualifies at |dv/dt| ≥ 0.4 m/s² while moving faster than 2.0 m/s, and at least 50 such samples are needed. Acceptance requires a correlation of at least 0.3 and agreement between disjoint halves of the data.

| Outcome | Segments | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `INSUFFICIENT_MOTION` | 51 | the vehicle never accelerated decisively enough to reveal a heading |
| `LOW_HEADING_CONFIDENCE` | 2 | tilt is solid, the heading evidence is too weak to accept |
| `MAG_DISTURBED` | 7 | the magnetometer was the remaining heading candidate and is disturbed |
| `SUCCESS` | 94 | full 3-DOF alignment, checks passed |

- Full 3-DOF alignment in **94 of 154** segments (median confidence 0.17).
- Median correlation behind an accepted heading: **0.70**.
- Median transformed-gravity residual: 0.00°, against a 10° limit.

A refusal is a result. `INSUFFICIENT_MOTION` on a segment that never accelerated decisively is the correct output, not a failure to be tuned away.

## 8. A finding: residual Phase 2 synchronization error
The heading estimator needs an independent scalar speed. Phase 2 supplies `ref_velocity_mps`, interpolated from a synchronized vehicle stream — and its contract explicitly warns against assuming that synchronization is exact.

It is not. Correlating the phone's horizontal acceleration against dv/dt at zero lag gives essentially nothing (**0.02** on the first segment examined). Scanning the lag reveals a clear peak several seconds away, and correcting it lifts the correlation to **0.96**.

Across this run, **92** segments needed a non-zero shift: median magnitude **4.6 s**, median signed value 4.4 s, maximum 14.6 s.

Phase 3 therefore estimates the lag itself over ±15 s and requires the peak to stand above the median of the search curve, so a flat curve's argmax is not mistaken for a measurement. **Nothing is written back**: the lag is a measurement of leftover error, reported on `AlignmentQuality.forward_lag_s`, and the canonical Parquet is untouched.

Two consequences for Phase 4. First, any use of `ref_velocity_mps` as a time-aligned target needs this residual accounted for. Second, both signals are low-passed over 2 s before correlating, because the phone's horizontal specific force (median ≈1.2 m/s² on real segments) is dominated by road vibration while the vehicle's dv/dt (median ≈0.37 m/s²) is a sub-Hz quantity.

## 9. Timing, sampling and duplicate handling
Three rules from the Phase 2 contract, honoured in one place (`idr.frames.sources`):

- **Clock.** All differencing uses `analysis_time_s`, the clock Phase 2 established as continuous per stream. `source_time_s` is carried through for provenance. `timestamp_utc` is never used for arithmetic — Phase 2 could not resolve its timezone.
- **Sampling.** Nothing assumes 10 Hz. Rates are measured per segment, and every window in this phase is defined in seconds rather than samples.
- **Duplicates.** Phase 2 retains and flags duplicate timestamps, so Δt = 0 reaches the estimators legitimately. Those steps are not integrated across; they are counted and reported. Where strictly increasing time is needed, a stated policy (keep the first row of each timestamp in canonical order) is applied to a **derived copy**.

- Measured rate across the analysed segments: median 10.00 Hz, range 2.00–999.83 Hz.
- Segments flagged irregularly sampled: **4**.
- Duplicate-timestamp rows encountered: **147,946** (retained, never dropped).

## 10. Comparison with the dataset's own orientation columns
IO-VNBD carries `orientation_yaw/pitch/roll`. These are compared **descriptively only**. Phase 2 established that this dataset's axis labels are untrustworthy aliases, the columns' own convention is undocumented, and the reference frame they are expressed in is unstated. A disagreement is therefore not evidence that either side is wrong.

| Column | Segments | Median RMSE vs our estimate |
|---|---|---|
| `orientation_roll` | 145 | 131.6° |
| `orientation_pitch` | 145 | 86.1° |
| `orientation_yaw` | 145 | 108.7° |

The disagreement is large. That is the expected result, not a defect: our yaw is dead-reckoned from an arbitrary origin in most segments, and the dataset's columns are in an unstated frame with unverified axis semantics. **Neither series is ground truth for the other.**

## 11. Performance
- Mean **77 µs/sample**, p95 **123 µs/sample** across 154 segments.
- Mean 1.07 s per segment, p95 5.70 s.

Recorded because the engine must eventually run in real time, **not** because anything here is optimized. Nothing in Phase 3 has been tuned for speed, and the orientation filter is a plain Python loop over samples. These numbers are a baseline to improve against, not a result.

## 13. Limitations and unresolved ambiguities
- **The phone frame's physical meaning is unverified.** Canonical `x/y/z` are column positions established by Phase 2. Whether they correspond to Android's runtime convention (X right, Y toward the top of the screen, Z out of the screen) is not stated by the dataset and cannot be established from it. Everything here is invariant to that question — the alignment is estimated from the data — but a future claim about physical axis meaning would need evidence this dataset does not contain.
- **The gyroscope's yaw/pitch/roll labels remain aliases.** Phase 2 verified that 158 files label these columns `Yaw/Pitch/Roll` and 73 label the same positions `X/Y/Z` with byte-identical rows. Phase 3 uses the positional mapping and never reads physical meaning from the labels.
- **No alignment ground truth exists.** Real-data results show self-consistency and plausibility, never accuracy.
- **Yaw is usually dead-reckoned.** With the magnetometer excluded in most segments, absolute heading drifts. This is reported per segment, not hidden.
- **The residual synchronization lag is measured per segment, not explained.** Whether it is vehicle-logger latency, a Phase 2 offset residual, or drift is not established here.
- **`timestamp_utc` remains timezone-unresolved** and is used for nothing.
- **Tilt from a moving-only segment can be biased** by the perpendicular-acceleration blind spot in section 4.
- **No INS, EKF, UKF, NHC, map matching, GNSS fusion, ML training, Android, ONNX, or outage simulation** is implemented, by design.

## 14. What Phase 4 may rely on
- `AlignmentEstimate.rotation` is `R_vehicle_phone` and is **`None` whenever yaw was not resolved**. Check it rather than assuming a rotation exists.
- `AlignmentEstimate.to_vehicle_frame(series)` raises rather than applying an unresolved alignment.
- `OrientationTrack` exposes per-axis support (`OBSERVED` / `DEAD_RECKONED` / `UNRESOLVED`) and a per-sample confidence.
- `AlignmentQuality` keeps the individual metrics — gravity residual, forward correlation, longitudinal and lateral correlation, angular-rate consistency, magnetometer weight, sample counts, and the residual lag. None of them is collapsed into an unexplained score.
- `CalibrationSession` accepts samples one at a time or in blocks; the two paths are asserted equivalent by test.
- Raw and canonical Phase 2 data are read-only throughout.
